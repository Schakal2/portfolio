<Chapter n="01" label="Context" />

<Lead>
A live store, a list of tickets and a platform I had never used before.
</Lead>

meinTee sells tea sets and gifts, with a configurator for engraved sets. When I took over the theme, the store was already selling every day, and content was being edited in the theme editor at the same time. I had not worked with Shopify before.

At first glance the tasks looked technical. Looking closer, almost every one came down to the same question: where do customers stumble, and how much trust does it cost?

<Chapter n="02" label="From ticket to usability" />

## Reviews: a copy bug becomes a better flow

The ticket said: the review form uses the formal German “Sie”, but meinTee talks to customers informally. Clicking through it, the tone was the smallest problem. The form was spread over several steps, and there was no X to close it. Anyone who just wanted to leave a star rating had to click through or leave the page.

<Quote>
Leaving a review is a favour to the store. Every extra click is a reason not to.
</Quote>

I made the case in the team for switching to the review app's single-page form. Because the app does not let you edit its texts, a small script swaps all 71 strings before they render, and I added a close button. The result: one page, in the brand's voice, that you can leave at any time.

<Chapter n="03" label="Learning Shopify without risking the store" />

## A routine instead of gut feeling

New to a platform and working on a live store: that only works with rules. I set up a fixed routine so no fix overwrites someone else's work and every customisation survives the next theme update. I am now working on making it the team's standard.

<Steps>
  <Step n="01" title="Pull the live theme">Download the code straight from the store, not from an old copy.</Step>
  <Step n="02" title="Compare">Diff every file against the original theme. This is how I learned Shopify from the inside.</Step>
  <Step n="03" title="Test in the browser">Prototype each fix in DevTools on the real store first.</Step>
  <Step n="04" title="Mark it">Tag every custom change so it survives updates.</Step>
  <Step n="05" title="Ship one file">One file at a time, after checking for parallel changes.</Step>
</Steps>

<Chapter n="04" label="Motion with a purpose" />

## A logo that assembles itself

Next to the tickets I built my own animations. The first one runs at the top of this page: the meinTee mark is split into three SVG layers that come together on the first visit. It runs on CSS keyframes only, no library, and respects “reduce motion”.

## Leaves that make room for the text

In the newsletter section, leaves fly in as you scroll. On phones and iPads they used to land right on top of the text. Now they measure the free space, move around the text and scale down. If there is not enough room, they drop out.
