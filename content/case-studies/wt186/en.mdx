## Background & Problem Statement

<Callout>  
Imagine this: You want to craft a lush pad sound, click through submenus of 50 identical knobs for 10 minutes — and lose your creative spark.
</Callout>

Audio synthesis is often abstract mathematics. But musicians don't want to think like engineers when creating — they want to immediately see and feel what generates a sound.

WT-186 solves this problem through drag-and-drop elevation data (like Mount Everest), turning geographic landscapes into dynamic soundscapes.

## The 2 Core Perspectives: The Dual-View Feedback Loop

### 1. The Sonar LCD: Precision in Radar Aesthetics
A compact display with crosshairs tracks the exact scanning position on the terrain surface. Radar aesthetics without overwhelming users with raw numbers.

## The "So What?" Comparator: Design Explorations & Friction Points

<Exploration title="Speed Control 1: Single knob for orbital speed" selected={false}>
The parameter range was ultra-sensitive — tiny tweaks immediately resulted in extreme noise or silence.
</Exploration>

<Exploration title="Speed Control 2: Split coarse & fine adjustment" selected={true}>
Allows both rapid leaps and subtle, highly musical tuning.
</Exploration>

<Exploration title="Modulation Matrix 1: Bulky grid table" selected={false}>
Overwhelms the eye and pulls producers out of their creative workflow.
</Exploration>

<Exploration title="Modulation Matrix 2: Hover-based light feedback" selected={true}>
Modulatable knobs gently glow on hover to communicate active LFOs directly on the control.
</Exploration>

## User Testing & Validation

Validation evolved from informal synth jams to targeted usability tests:
- Are drag affordances on knobs clear without instructions?
- Do producers grasp modulation state without opening submenus?
- Does the hover lighting model perform reliably across different displays?

---

## Key Takeaways

<Callout>
**Great interface design removes friction between humans and technology.**
Complex algorithms reveal their true magic when the interface doesn't force musicians to think like engineers, but lets them experiment intuitively and visually with sound.
</Callout>
