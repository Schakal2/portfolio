## Splitting costs without giving away your data

On trips and in families, someone always pays for everyone. Splitwise solves that, but it wants an account from every person and builds a business on their data. I wanted the same core idea without the overhead: open a link, enter what you paid, see who owes whom.

The first version was for my family. Its real test came with six friends on a trip to Venice, all on their phones, in shops and restaurants, often with one hand.

---

## Design decisions

- **The answer comes first.** The dark card at the top answers the only question that matters: who gets money back, and who owes how much. Everything else sits below it.
- **Fewer payments to settle up.** Next to the person-to-person balances, Evenly suggests a simplified plan. In the example, 4 payments settle what would otherwise take 9.
- **One tap marks a debt as paid.** A settlement is just another entry in the history, so nothing gets lost and everyone can see it.
- **A personal view.** Each person can open their own balance: what they paid, what their share was, and what is still open. Every line explains itself, for example "paid by Jonas, your share (÷5)".
- **No sign up.** The group shares one link. An optional password keeps strangers out. The app remembers on each phone who you are.

## Built for one hand and a busy table

Entering an expense takes four fields: what, how much, who paid, and who shares. Everyone is selected by default, because that is the common case. Amounts accept a comma or a dot. Errors are written as plain requests, like "Please say briefly what it was for."

## Small stack, honest maths

The whole interface is one HTML file. A single Vercel function stores people and expenses in Upstash Redis, and every phone checks for changes every 15 seconds and when the app comes back to the foreground.

All amounts are stored in cents. When a bill does not divide evenly, the leftover cents are assigned one by one, so the balances always add up to exactly zero.

---

## From joke name to product name

The app started as "Julianwise", a nod to Splitwise. For the portfolio I gave it a proper identity: **Evenly** says what it does. The mark is an equals sign in the brand amber, which also works as the home screen icon. The group name now comes from a setting instead of being hard coded, so the same app works for a trip, a shared flat or a family.

---

## Takeaways

- **Real use beats any test.** Once the group used it every day, it became clear what was missing. The personal balance view came out of that.
- **Small is a feature.** One file and one function are easy to understand, cheap to host and quick to change on the go.
- **Explain the numbers.** People trust a balance when every line says where it comes from.
